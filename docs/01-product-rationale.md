# 01 · Product rationale

## The one-sentence promise

**No Global Year student should feel unseen, unsupported, or forgotten while they are away from home.**

Everything else in this document is that sentence, applied.

## The problem, concretely

A Global Year student spends September to May in Guatemala, Cape Verde, Mexico, Italy, or Southeast Asia — in language school every morning, in ministry every afternoon, and far from every person who loves them. Around that student stands a community that genuinely wants to help but consistently doesn't know *how*:

- Parents wonder how their kid is really doing and don't want to be the fifth person asking today.
- Church members would pray specifically if they knew what to pray for; instead they pray "for the missionaries" in general.
- A small group would happily cover $60 of care-package postage — if anyone had told them it was needed.
- Coordinators sense when a student is struggling but find out weeks late, from a parent, secondhand.
- And the student, who raised $15,000 of faith support to get there, feels the quiet pressure of being everyone's investment and no one's neighbor.

The result is a support gap made of goodwill: everyone cares, nothing lands.

## The core design bet

**Support fails not from lack of love but from lack of clarity.** So the app's job is to convert love into *specific, visible, coordinated action* — one prayer, one need, one note at a time — without ever turning the student into a project, a campaign, or a dashboard metric.

This borrows one idea from the "Just Ask" direction: a need that is visible and specific gets met. Kindred generalizes it — needs, prayer requests, milestones, and even a student's emotional weather are all made *appropriately* visible, to *chosen* people, so that helping is never a guess.

## How the design keeps the student at the center

**1. The student owns the audience of everything.**
Every update, need, prayer request, and check-in carries a visibility choice drawn from the student's support circles. There is no "public." The most open setting — "All supporters" — still means *people the student individually approved*. The prototype implements this literally: switch from Dana (family circle) to Jordan (friends circle) and Elijah's honest reflection disappears.

**2. Wellbeing is a door the student opens, never a window others look through.**
Check-ins default to private. When a student selects something heavy — homesick, overwhelmed, need support — the app offers *options*, not automation: tell a mentor, alert a care team, turn it into a prayer request, or keep it private. The coordinator's "support health" view is built only from engagement signals (unanswered needs, encouragement drought, milestone proximity) — never from private content. Pastor Marcus can see that Elijah *could use care*; he cannot see why.

**3. Money is present but never the plot.**
Students are faith-supported; ignoring finances would be dishonest. But the student's overall funding number is private by default ("Quiet progress · Visible to: Only me"), financial needs can be scoped to a financial-supporters circle, giving can be anonymous, and there are no thermometers on profiles, no leaderboards, no countdown pressure. Sarah's flight deadline is framed as a *milestone her community shows up for*, not a campaign target.

**4. The profile is a story, not a statement of account.**
The hero of the profile is "Why I'm doing Global Year" — the grandmother's stories, the girl in Praia who asked "when are you coming back?" Supporter counts and prayer counts appear as warmth ("318 prayers"), never as rank. Two students are never compared; two supporters are never compared.

**5. Encouragement is designed as a keepsake, not a feed.**
The Encouragement Wall collects every note, verse, voice message, and photo from home in one place the student can return to on a hard night. Scheduled delivery ("land it on her birthday") turns supporter intention into student experience at exactly the right moment.

## How the design makes support simple and visible for everyone else

**Supporters never have to wonder how to help.** The Home screen answers it directly: *Today's Support Moment* (one student, one action), needs you can help with, prayers waiting, milestones coming. Every need offers graded on-ramps — "I'll pray," "I can contribute," "I can cover this," "I know someone who can help" — so a retiree, a college roommate, and a grandparent all find a first step sized to them.

**Commitments are coordinated, not duplicated.** When someone offers help, the flow is explicit: what exactly, one-time or ongoing, deadline, reminders, whether the student needs to confirm. "3 people have contributed" and "Help is already on the way" prevent both pile-on and gaps. Backing out is shame-free by design ("thanks for telling us early so others can step in").

**Prayer is the first-class citizen.** The Prayer tab is deliberately the calmest surface in the app — a daily feed, a big "I prayed" moment with haptic warmth, optional notes to the student, private notes for the supporter, and answered-prayer celebrations that close the loop. "This request has been prayed for by 18 people" tells a lonely student in Antigua something a bank balance never could.

**Coordinators get orchestration without surveillance.** The dashboard surfaces which students need care, which face milestones, and which are thriving — then gives one-tap plays: ask three supporters to reach out, schedule a group prayer moment, rally the church around a flight deadline. The care response is invisible to the student as a *mechanism*; they just experience more of their people showing up.

## What we deliberately did not build

- No public directory or search — connection starts with a student-issued invite.
- No real-time location — location is country/city/region-level and student-configured, always.
- No streaks, badges, leaderboards, or guilt notifications ("You haven't checked in with Elijah recently" is a nudge with an easy action, capped in frequency, and never framed as failure).
- No donation checkout dominating the UI — giving flows exist, but the app leads with presence, prayer, and encouragement.
- No admin omniscience — Global Year staff verify accounts and see anonymized health metrics; they never automatically see private prayer or wellbeing content.

## Why this wins

Retention in a support app is emotional, not mechanical. A supporter who prays for a specific request and later sees "**this prayer has been answered**" has completed a loop no generic newsletter can offer — they were *part of it*. A student who opens the Encouragement Wall on a homesick Tuesday and finds their mom's voice, their pastor's note, and fourteen people praying is a student who finishes their year. The app succeeds when the student feels personally known, consistently supported, and spiritually covered — and when every supporter can say: helping was clear, meaningful, and something I could do today.
